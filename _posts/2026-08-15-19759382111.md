Today, Clarabel and I embarked on my final little leg of the journey; reaching the Atlantic Ocean. To get there, we biked through Central Park, across Manhattan, over the East River, and through numerous city centers until reaching Rockaway Beach. It felt like we traversed across multiple countries in the span of those 25 miles. Streets completely packed with cars, intersections held at standstills with bikes and pedestrians weaving through the mess, roads shut down to traffic and open to runners, and cars double-parked along entire blocks. Biking in this city requires 110% focus to stay aware of your surroundings but it is such a cool way to see other neighborhoods.

Once we arrived at the beach and I dismounted my bike for the final time to jump in the Atlantic Ocean, my trip was officially complete. I had planned to dip my front tire in the water, but the shear number of people lying on this beach was too overwhelming to imagine wheeling a bike between all the tanning bodies. Instead, I nearly broke my wrist in my attempt to hoist my bike above my head in victory formation.

For my final entry, I wanted to share some thoughts I’ve collected throughout the ride. I’ve tried my best to synthesize these into something reminiscent of a lessons learned and closing remarks for everyone who cares to read.

First and foremost, I owe much of my success to all of you reading these posts and supporting me along the way. Whether it was emotional support cheering me on in person or from afar through the inter-webs, all of the people I’ve met along the way who shared their stories and wished me luck, the kind souls who opened their homes and welcomed me for the night, or everyone who was so generous to financially support me; thank you. This trip wouldn't have been possible without you.

My biggest take away from the trip is reminding myself that I always have a choice. Life is full of paths (humor me as I attempt this metaphor). Sometimes we find ourselves at a crossroads where we are forced to make a decision. Other times, we realize indecision has led us further down a path than originally intended. These paths may run parallel to the one you are currently on, others may cross over, and some may be a complete U-turn. Though you may be able to peek over the trees and see parts of what that new path holds, you never know what turns of its own are beyond your view. Whether it’s comfort, fear, or laziness keeping you on your current path, I urge you to take a chance and deviate. Even if only for a quick detour. Do it just to remind yourself how big the world is, how many choices you have, and how much control you have over your direction. You always have choices. As they say, the world is your oyster and there’s a great happy hour right down the street selling them for a dollar a pop.

My other, slightly more stereotypical lesson to share, is to trust in the universe to take care of you. If not the universe, then trust in the people. Popular opinion would have us believe that we have become so different and polarized from each other. However, when you find yourself physically face to face, treating people with love and respect, you come to realize that we are all fundamentally the same. Just as you would care for someone else, they will care for you. Every time I found myself in a pinch, the solution seemed to present itself organically, and the end result was often better than I could have ever imagined. I just had to keep an open mind, stay flexible, and be willing to try something new.

Looking back, some of my favorite memories from the trip include biking past herding animals who joined together to gallop beside me, fields full of budding grass catching the evening sun and casting an amber glow, alpine lakes still and flat reflecting a perfect image of the mountain range beyond, shared meals that turned strangers into friends, the curiosity in people’s eyes when they asked about my trip, and the nights spent under the stars.

On the flip side, some of my least favorite things were headwinds, fresh roadkill, tornadoes, angry drivers, narrow road shoulders, and dairy farms. So as I wrap up this final entry, my request to you all is simple: please don’t open a dairy farm in my town, don’t drive angry, and be an advocate for cyclists in your neck of the woods.

Thank you all for being part of this ride.
