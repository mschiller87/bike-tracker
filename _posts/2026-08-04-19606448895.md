I started the day with a sleep score of 98! I’m not sure how much I believe in Garmin’s ability to accurately assess my sleep but I felt a confidence boost seeing that high score. The gang got moving much earlier than yesterday, hitting the road around 9am.

The first stop of the day was at “The Little Pie Shop” for breakfast. Austin and I each got the chorizo breakfast bowl special. It turned out to be an utterly enormous mountain of chorizo sausage gravy, eggs, potato, and cheese. Way too much food, even for a hungry biker to eat. This did not stop me from trying, even after eating a slice of cherry pie as an appetizer.

Along the ride today, we discovered that I did not know how to pronounce the word peculiar. After hundreds of repetitions in my head and out loud, I can now pronounce it and it has completely lost all its meaning to me. We had a flat tire scare with Dom’s bike. We took a loooooong break at a scenic overlook. We stopped at a roadside fruit stand where I housed a whole carton of blueberries. Then we cranked the last 20 miles of the ride into Erie, PA.

Our first stop in town was at a dinner for something fried and something frozen, onion rings and a caramel sundae were my choices. A long beach session on Presque Isle and a swim in Erie left me feeling sun-kissed and ready for a shower.

We made it over to a cute tapas restaurant for the final meal as a three pack. The food was great and provided healthy nutrients that double cheeseburgers cannot provide. I said my goodbyes to Dom and Austin before heading to my campsite. It was so nice having their company for this section. I doubt I would have been able to squeeze as much enjoyment out of the tourist town of Geneva-by-the-lake or the beach town of Erie without them.

I realized that tonight was my first night camping since Nebraska… For my first night back on the ground, I pitched the tent in a pretty unique location. Sarah’s campground is an old spot that allows tents directly on the beach with private access to the water. A 25 cent shower got the stick off my skin and set me up for a nice night of sleep on Erie coast.
