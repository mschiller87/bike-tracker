Slow start to the morning leaving the Shady Spruce Hostel in Missoula. We stopped by and chatted with Jeff at Adventure Cycling Association (the organization that builds and organizes the network of cycling maps I used to build this route). It was awesome to hear about all the history of bicycle touring in America. The coolest story we heard was that of 25 year old Frank Lenz from Pittsburg. In 1892 he was the first documented person to tour through Missoula on a bike. He rode a single speed bike with his camera equipment and the bare essentials weighing in over 110 lbs. He crossed the country in 107 days, averaging 45 miles per day (not that much slower than in doing right now). He went on to travel all across the world, unimaginable with the lack of technology and rudimentary gear of the time.

The biking today was pretty uneventful. I spent some time at the Bitterroot Public Library making some changes to my planned route (less milage, less mountain passes, less dogs, more services, better). Then I grabbed a beer and dinner at Bitter Root Brewing. Their flagship beer is the Dirt Church Hazy. Their merch says, “Biking solves most of my problems, Dirt Church solves the rest”, and I really felt that… I ended up spending more money at this brewery than I have for any other meal this trip and it was worth every penny.
