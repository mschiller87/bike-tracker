Today was meant to be an easy day. The plan was to pop in to all the popular scenic spots and wind up at camp a little ways down the road. Over time, “a little ways down the road” has grown and grown and grown. Today, it meant almost 50 miles and crossing the continental divide 3 times (in simple English, lots of climbing).

Despite the intensity of the biking, I was able to see the top two places on my list: the prismatic pools and Old Faithful. I learned that the array of colors you can see in the pools is created by the bacteria in the water and the variations in color are due to the water temperature differences. Red streaks exist where water temps are around 120F, the darker blue areas exist in water temps between 160-180F. Pretty hot. Old Faithful was a trip, the geyser was cool, but I was more entertained by the THOUSANDS of people gathered around this thing like a sporting event awaiting the blast. The park has a time table, down to the minute, telling you when it with erupt next. However, the lack of patience of the average American was put on full display as people grew tired of standing and asked for the third time, “when is it supposed to happen?”

Yellowstone could use a larger shoulder for bikes and RV drivers should require additional training before being allowed on the road.

After the roaring success of last night’s dinner, I splurged on a metal bowl that should allow me to more easily cook some higher quality meals. Tonight for dinner, I made broccoli cheddar rice with a fresh red pepper. I know this may not sound like much to all of you folks at home, but my body celebrates every fresh vegetable I can get in me. Plus, this recipe included a secret ingredient, the rice was boiled in water scooped straight out of Lewis Lake on the dock the meal was cooked on. Call it, “lake to table”.

I spent the evening lounging on the dock in the sun and breeze to escape the mosquito nightmare that awaited me back at my tent. If you read this far and scrolled through all the photos from today, you deserve an award. Thanks for following along.
