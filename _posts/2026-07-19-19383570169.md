I said goodbye to my friend Mike this morning. I’ve been riding with him since we crossed paths way back in eastern Oregon a month and a half ago. He will continue on east on a diagonal path towards Boston. My path takes me north along the old Route 66 before turning east towards NYC. There’s a chance the stars align and we see each other again along the Erie Canal.

I snuck out of Pete and Jordan’s house early this morning, hitting the trails out of Edwardsville at 5:30am. Today was bound to be a big one with almost 90 miles separating me from Springfield, IL. At some point, I may have inadvertently made a deal with the devil, trading cooler temps with overcast clouds, in exchange for headwinds all day. Unbeknownst to me at the beginning of the day, these headwinds were also expediting the delivering of the Canadian wildfire smoke sooner than expected.

It was a slow transition from overcast gloom, to wildfire smoke. I thought I had a few days separating me from the smoke, but I slowly realized that the haziness in the air was not the humidity or morning mist I was used to. The wind is supposed to change direction tomorrow so hopefully that means no smoke and tailwinds.

I chipped away at today’s ride in 20 mile increments. I’ve learned that the best way to visualize the remaining ride is to completely forget what ground I’ve already covered. It sounds way better to think “I only have 47 miles left, that’s a short day”, instead of thinking, “I’ve gone 40 miles and I’m not even halfway done yet”. This approach relies on thoughtful disillusionment, it assumes after covering 40 miles, I’m still as fresh as I was at the start of the day, and another 47 will be a piece of cake. It’s worked so far, I try not to think about it too hard and just take it one 10-20 mile chunk at a time.

I got to Springfield around 1pm. I strolled by the capitol building and Abe Lincoln’s childhood home, before heading to a bar to watch the World Cup finals. I befriended the bartender who was excited to hear about my trip and let me bring my bike into the bar. He proceeded to over-serve me heavily discounted and free beer because he said I needed the calories.

I arrived at Karl’s house for the night. He took me out to dinner with his partner Chris, then showed me around the Illinois Fairground. It was great hearing all of his biking and historical stories. He is planning to join me for a few miles on tomorrow’s ride!
