Karl joined me for the first 10 miles of today’s ride. He led me along a new section of bike trail that isn’t shown on the maps I’ve been using to navigate. Trading slightly more milage for the absence of cars is almost always a good trade.

After the bike trail ended, I was stuck on bypass and frontage roads that used to be the old Route 66. These roads are incredibly beat up and horrible to bike on. I think bumps are the hardest part about bike touring. Here is my ranking of the worst things you can experience while out on the road (most worst to least worst): Bumps, humidity, traffic, storms, heat, hills. Combine two of these and you’re probably not stoked, combine three of these and you question how you ended up in this situation. If you find yourself simultaneously experiencing four or more of these, you should probably get out of Missouri.

The bumps on the frontage roads made me envious of the smooth pavement on I-55 off to my side. Out west, it wouldn’t be a big deal to bike on the interstate, but suddenly, I’ve found myself in territory where it is, “strictly illegal”.

I made a few stops in the various towns along the way today. Williamsville for Casey’s, Lincoln for Mel-O-Cream Donuts (a Route 66 staple), Atlanta for a pin and some photos of the big men, McLean for the famous Route 66 Dixie truck stop restaurant. When I arrived in Bloomington, I spent some time in the new public library finally giving some love to my book I’ve carried this whole way. When I left the library, I enjoyed a light drizzle while riding on the Constitution Trail to Kellie’s house. I’ve only biked along a 5 mile stretch of the trail, but after seeing the maps and experiencing the quality, I would rank Bloomington on a similar scale as Eugene, OR in terms of bike-friendly cities. I wish all cities showed bikers this kind of love.

To be completely honest, this was the section of my trip I was least looking forward to. I figured it would be more of the same scenery just along busier highways. To some degree, this is true. Most of my views are corn on the left, corn on the right, or corn on both sides. However, there’s an added element of history in each of the small towns along Route 66 that adds more flair than I was expecting. Old neon signs, vibrant murals, strange statues of massive men, old building and monuments, and historic restaurants that have stood the test of time over the last 100 years. Similar to all the other roads I’ve biked, it’s always going to suck a little, but if you push past that, there’s a lot of beauty to experience along the way.
