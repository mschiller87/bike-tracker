Today started with a torrential downpour. Luckily, we hadn’t left the RV yet. After half an hour of rain and lightning, the storm passed and left us with slightly cooler temps and shade for the morning.

We unceremoniously crossed into Kansas, I guess not enough people enter the state along this route to warrant a welcome sign. I only knew I was in Kansas after passing a billboard that read, “please don’t hit Kansas road workers”.

Today, the roads were smooth and the wind was minimal. I may even go so far as to say the conditions were good. The highs only reached 89F which is still quite uncomfortable, but felt worlds better than 95F. I think I felt stronger in the heat because I hydrated better and consumed copious amounts of electrolytes. Who knew all it took to survive out here was one liquid IV, one pedialyte, and two nuun tablets.

It sounds like we may be relying on Casey’s for most of our hot food for the next few days as these small towns don’t always have any other dinning options. I am not upset about it. Today I had their breakfast pizza, as recommended by @lizzie, and was blown away. The pizza did not look very visually exciting; Cheese, sausage, and egg didn’t exactly provide much of a color pop. However, the magic was under the cheese. The pizza had a sausage gravy base that was savory and buttery with a hint of sage. I highly recommend it.

We passed by the center of America. I celebrated by pointing to the center of Matt. This lined up almost perfectly with the halfway point on my trail 2,150 miles! The second half of the trip should move quicker than the first with higher milage days, less climbing, lower elevation.

I was able to sweet talk us into the basement of the local Methodist church here in Mankato (pronounced with a hard midwestern A [man-kay-toe]). We fell into our usual routine like clockwork after securing the church. Find a cold drink, drop off the bikes, head to the public pool, find dinner, crash. A huge bonus at this church was the 3 couches in the back room. I’ve grown used to sitting in the gravel on the side of roads, picnic benches, and my leather bike seat. I feel luxurious when I pull out my small foam pad to provide a little cushion. A couch feels like Heaven when you’re this deprived.
