Today was a blast. It was 30 miles of uphill, then 70 miles of downhill. First Benjamin ride in the books. To say I bombed the hill would be an understatement. I had access to the launch codes and when completely nuclear down that canyon. My fastest five mile split was over 30mph average speed. My top speed was 46mph. I felt like I had wings.

The day didn’t start as good as it ended. I got going early, hitting the road at 6am. Within the first 10 miles, a tendon in my right knee was screaming at me. I made some adjustments to my cleat that seemed to reduce the pain, but it had already swollen up and hurt to pedal. I was really doubting my ability to make it to the top of Cameron Pass, let alone the 100 miles to Fort Collins. Every truck and RV that passed by me as a potential escape route that crossed my mind. After a few more miles and confidence that the pain wasn’t increasing, I took some ibuprofen to reduce the swelling and told myself that if any sharp pains surfaced, I could call it quits. Either my ibuprofen was super strength or my adjustments were perfect, because I went the rest of the day pain free.

My only stops along the way were breathers going uphill, photo stops, hot dog stop🌭, and a beer break (for the carbs). I got the beer at the Mishawaka, a really cool restaurant and music venue. It felt like the smaller, more underground version of red rocks. If you’re in the area I think this place is worth checking out.

Biking through the Poudre Canyon was hands down the best ride of the trip so far. Debatably, the best ride of my life, so far. The road was butter, the grade was delicious, the traffic was friendly, and the views were enchanting. 10/10 would do again (either grind the ride up or hitch hike to the top of the pass).

I rolled in to Fort Collins and hit 99 miles. I’m against the idea of adding an additional mile just to hit a special number. However, after running some errands, I happened to cross the century threshold. My legs are a little jelly feeling but for the most part I feel pretty good. Maybe next time I’m ready for a big rip I need to set my sights longer.
