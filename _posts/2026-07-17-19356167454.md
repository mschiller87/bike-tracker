A nice cool early morning start gave me the boost to hammer out 30 miles to get to the closest grocery store. I was experiencing the early symptoms of a bonk as we parked our bikes and headed in. The freezing cold AC snapped me back into reality and helped me make some educated decisions while shopping.

For brunch I treated myself to a bratwurst, a peach, a red bell pepper, an orange juice, and a blackberry pie. Eating this array of food in the grocery store lobby, while Mike sawed the top off his milk jug to use as a cereal bowl, brought out some uncontrolled laughter from me. If you’ve never experienced this moment before, it comes on every long distance trip I’ve ever been on. You get comfortable enough with the people you’re with, and after a long time in the wilderness or in a state of disarray, you descend into hysteria and no longer know how to act in public. Today, I crossed that line.

The cool weather was gone by the time we left the grocery store. For those of you who know me well, you know that one of my least favorite human experiences is sweating. I’ve been forced to face my demons out here and accept the fact that I will be drenched every single day in this humidity. I’ve been setting all sorts of records on this trip, sometimes they are in categories that Strava doesn’t capture. Today, I broke my personal record for being the stinkiest I’ve ever been. I’m worried that a residential washing machine may not be strong enough to remove this stench.

For lunch, we stopped and got food a the KT Caboose. A trail side restaurant serving food out of an old train caboose. The owner, Doctor John, was an inspiring dude who has done some crazy trips of his own. Most notably, his run across America, from Key West to San Diego where he averaged 40 miles/day. He fixed us up with a delicious 3 course lunch for $12.

After a good meal, with only 19 miles left, I plugged in my headphones and played Tame Impala’s record, InnerSpeaker. This album must have been recorded with this exact use case in mind because it sent me into orbit and I blasted those last miles at mock speed. I no longer felt the heat, my skin had goosebumps, and my heart rate was steady. I imagine this is what good steroids feel like and I understand why people like them.

I returned to earth when I arrived in Defiance, MO and was quickly transformed into a steaming puddle of salt water. We moseyed up an 18% hill to our bed and breakfast for the night and I immediately hopped into a cold shower and put myself down for a nap.

The scenery today was strangely post-apocalyptic. It was hot as death, there were hardly any other humans, it’s just a single gravel path surrounded by thick overgrown forest, and an occasional abandoned car overtaken by rust and vines. I suppose the image is lost when you consider the grocery at mile 20, the smoothies at mile 55, and the bed a breakfast at mile 73. I miss Casey’s.
