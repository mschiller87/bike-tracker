I said goodbye to my dad this morning before hitting the road at 6am. The temperature was delightful all morning, I wish he was still biking with us.

I mapped out a new route for today’s ride, different from the published route from Adventure Cycling Association (the map I’ve been following for most of the trip). In the past, I’ve done this when there is a town or attraction I want to check out, and it usually results in busier, bumpier roads. The route they choose are chosen for good reason. However, today I think I chose a better one. The road was buttery smooth with hardly any cars. The re-route also shaved off 14 miles and over 1000 feet of climbing.

In addition to the cool temps this morning, I also thoroughly enjoyed the reintroduction of trees to the landscape. The past three states have been nothing but straight roads and crop fields, aka no where to hide from the sun. Here in Missouri, the roads wind through lush forests with tree towering over the pavement creating a tunnel like canopy casting shade all day long. The air smells like life again, not poop. Each breath also feels like a small sip of water with this crazy humidity. Sink laundry has become a requirement with the amount of sweat I’m creating each day.

I made a big upgrade to my bike setup. I sent my rear panniers home with my dad along with some gear I’ve deemed less important. That leaves just my front fork bags, handlebar camera bag, frame bag and a single bag strapped to my rear rack. My bike has converted from a touring bike into a space ship. I feel so fast and light on this thing now. The only downsides to this weight shedding is that my sleeping pad is much less comfortable, and I’m running with almost no spare clothing. Worthy sacrifices if you ask me.
