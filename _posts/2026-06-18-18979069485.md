Started the day same as most, with 2 packets of oatmeal. The special side quest this morning was dipping my feet in a hot spring right on the edge of town. The water must have been over 105F because after a couple minutes, my feet felt burned. Not sure if this was a healthy decision but I’m happy to have made it.

I covered two passes today, Big Hole Pass and Badger Pass. Both were fairly mild climbs, but the descents were primo. I ended at a lower elevation than I started today which means more time was spent going down hill and it was FELT. I was clocking speeds over 40mph going down some of these sections. Going fast down hill, the wind transforms into a sort of music. At the bottom, I’m left feeling like I just heard a great song with a strong desire to click replay.

At one point, I saw two bikers on the horizon and realized I was gaining on them (which was strange because it didn’t look like they had any baggage on their bikes). They turned out to be competing in the continental divide race. These guys were riding single speed bikes, averaging 130 miles and 10k feet of climbing per day with only 4 hours of sleep each night. Just when you start to think you’re pretty cool, you discover that the world is full of people who do what you do in their sleep. These guys were looking forward to their first shower in 7 days while I was looking forward to the Mexican Food truck in the next town over. Props to Geoff and Tanner, good luck on the rest of the race!

 I’m staying at a biker resort in Dillon MT tonight. This guy built a few cabins has has them kitted out with a full bike mechanic work bench, a mini kitchen, showers, and plenty of indoor/outdoor places to camp. Spending time in these unique places has been one of the best parts of the trip. The smallest amenities mean so much, any night that I don’t need to pitch my tent feels like a win.

I spent the better part of the afternoon laid out on a lounge chair in the shade listening to music. This is exactly what the doctor ordered.
