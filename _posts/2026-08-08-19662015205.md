I slept in until 6:30 this morning. I knew I didn’t have 100 miles to bike today so I tried to relax a bit and slow down the pace.

The sun was out this morning and it completely changed the scenery. The greens of the grasses came to life, and the murky water turned to a pristine reflecting pool (no nano-bubblers required).

I had my copilot (Clarabel) on the phone and she led me to a spot for breakfast. They served up freshly baked everything bagels and some freshly fried donuts. I had pizza lunch again today, this time from a gas station. Not just any old gas station though, Stewart’s Shops is quickly becoming the new Casey’s. Most towns have at least one location and the goods/services they offer are consistently quantity. I’ve been told that their ice cream is on another level, so that has become tomorrow’s mission.

I passed a section of the old canal that was overtaken with algae. Upon further investigation, I realized that nearly every exposed log was covered in turtles. Some logs had 10+ turtles on them!! It made me think of the movie Human Centipede and I nearly got sick thinking about it. If you don’t know what I’m talking about, you are leading a much healthier lifestyle and I beg you to not look it up. Anyways, the turtles were cute but hard to photograph. Every time I stopped my bike, they abandoned ship and hid.

I was impacted by the ever disappointing weather forecast again today when a surprise downpour soaked me to the bone with less than two miles left for the day. Luckily, the campsite for the night was the marina in Little Falls, NY, and they had an indoor lounge to dry off in. Here I was reunited with my friend Mike and we met Tom who was working at the marina. Tom would go on to lead us on an evening crusade of the small town of Little Falls that I do not have the energy to recount in heavy detail. Some highlights included driving 4 laps around town in Tom’s car, trying Riggies (infamous local pasta dish), meeting the entire family who owned the restaurant, and getting free dessert because we are now part of the family. It was a good night but dragged on much longer than I would have hoped, much to the fault of Mike being the friendliest most talkative man I’ve ever met.

Sometimes you just gotta release control of the reigns a little bit and let life take you for a ride. If I could do it again, I might not let 80 year old Tom drive us home after 4 drinks at the bar.
