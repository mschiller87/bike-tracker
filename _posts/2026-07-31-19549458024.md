Said goodbye to my parents this morning. The time always goes by so quick when I stay with them. My mom dropped me off at my brother’s house in Royal Oak where I ended my ride yesterday. As demonstrated by this coordination, I’ve become pretty attached to the concept of completing a continuous path of biking from coast to coast. At this point, even a small gap of a few miles that weren’t traveled by bicycle would haunt me at the end of the trip.

I was pleasantly surprised by the bike friendly infrastructure in Detroit. While in the city and the surrounding suburbs, I found myself in a dedicated bike path or protected lane more often than not. I stopped and did a lap on a pump track just outside of Ferndale. It was sketchier than I originally anticipated with all my baggage on the bike. Luckily, I got away on two wheels. As always, the old Tudor revival homes outside the city caught my eye. Most of the city feels very safe and coming back to life, despite what the news or your coworker who hasn’t been to Detroit since 1995 says. The riverfront offered some nostalgic views and was my first time returning to the area since I ran it in the 2025 Detroit marathon.

After passing through Detroit, the views returned to those typical of the rest of the Midwest; corn and soybean. That is, until the Detroit River spilled out into Lake Erie and my route kissed up to the shore line. Around this point, I crossed the state line into Ohio and rode over a bridge spanning the width of the Maumee River. This was around mile 75 of the ride and the caffeine from the tall boy Coca Cola was starting to kick in making me feel like an unstoppable fright trail. The surge was somewhat short lived, around mile 90 I was struggling to reel in my heart rate despite a leisurely pace. I think this has something to do what my time off. Obviously the heat and the distance played a role, but I’ve noticed that after some time off the bike, my body shifts out of mile-munching mode into something more like muscle-mending mode. Changing gears sometimes takes a few days and the switchover period is felt with higher heart rates and excessive sweating.

After the long day, I met Gordon at his home where he is so graciously hosting me for the night. His son and grandkids came over for a steak dinner and we all helped cook. I nervously watched as Gordon’s son stabbed the sweat potatoes with a chefs knife dangerously close to his fingers, but no blood was shed. I excused myself from the company around 8pm for an early bedtime as tomorrow I have an even bigger day ahead of me to get to Cleveland. 

There was a small voice in my head that told me to do an extra mile and a half to make it a clean 100 mile ride. Ultimately, I decided against it because any milage savings at this point is warmly welcomed in my book. I may regret this decision to pad my cycling stats when someone asks me how many century rides I’ve done, but I doubt it.
