Early morning start for the day. I was riding along the North Coast Inland Trail for most of the morning, a nice paved rail to trail path. The early morning was dark enough under the trees that I used my headlamp for the first time all trip. I got a French toast breakfast at a local diner after 20 miles, then pushed on.

Around mile 40, I noticed a slow moving train crossing the road a ways ahead of me. As I got closer it looked like the train was slowing down. It eventually came to a stop. All of the cars waiting to cross the tracks started turning around and leaving. I sat on a rock and waited for a bit, but a quick google search said that trains like this can remain stopped for hours as they wait for the tracks to open up. I peeked down the tracks and saw that the end of the train wasn’t too far away. I “biked” along the edge of the tracks over baseball sized rocks and all the other scrap you find along rail lines, crossed over at the end of the train, then double backed to the road.

I’m not sure if it’s the distance I’ve traveled or the similar scenery I’m passing through, but I’ve found myself getting somewhat bored while riding recently. I feel tired but I can’t point to any one body part or muscle that aches (besides my butt), and I feel like my brain might just be the most exhausted. I’m kind of leaning on the thought of just blasting out as many miles as I can to cross the finish line sooner.

Along route 6, I ran into Emma and Dan, father daughter combo who have made it their mission to walk across whole states. They are currently working on the state of Ohio. Dan was very eager to hear about my trip and it was great chatting with some fellow travelers.

As I approached Cleveland, I got some nice views of a cloudy Erie, saw some beautiful homes, got a fancy hot dog, and got rained on. I eventually arrived at my friend Nick’s house and had a great night hanging out with old friends. Nick offered up with room with freezing cold AC all night. I slept like a corpse for almost 10 hours.
