Today was a challenging day. I faced 30mph wind with 50mph gusts. It required me to ride with my bike at an angle to compensate for the wind. To help visualize this wind speed, I was able to spit clear across the three lane highway into the other shoulder with the wind assist.

I arrived at the big climb for the day. Before beginning to climb, I ate the last of my McDonald’s reserves, popped a gel, and plugged in my headphones with some fast paced EDM queued up. Then I started up the 5 mile 2,000 foot climb that would end up taking me almost 2 hours to complete. This climb was pretty typical compared to previous climbs on the trip, it wasn’t super steep and wasn’t crazy big, but the wind was a completely new factor. Much like the previous 25 miles, it blew directly at me or diagonally across my body the whole way up. In the more positive moments, it felt like I was flying a space ship through an asteroid field. Unpredictable gusts of wind forcing me to overcompensate my navigation of the broken glass and shards of metal on the side of the road while staying between the 24” strip of pavement with rumble strips to my left and a steep gravel bank to my right. In the more negative moments, it felt like hell.

It’s hard to describe what I went through on this climb, so I’ll just explain objectively what happened to me. I was struggling to keep my bike moving fast enough to maintain my balance. It required a level of continuous exertion I haven’t used on this ride so far. My heart rate rose above my previous zone 2 limits and kept me out of breath the whole time. The music allowed me to zone out and just grind up the hill, the beat of the song commanding my legs to keep pumping. However, when the song ended, I would typically find myself crying. I wasn’t really sure why and it kind of scared me. Between songs, I would return to my body and find that my fingers and toes had gone numb because I was so gripped trying to stay balanced while pushing so hard. The next song would start and I would lock back in and the cycle would continue. But I kept crying every few minutes, I have never experienced this before. I think it was a combination of fear and a general lack of control of my bodily functions. Fear of the wind and what it’s capable of, at times it sounds like music, but today it was overpowering and threatening. The thing that concerned me most about the tears was that I didn’t have that much water on me and didn’t want any more leaving my body on top of the sweat already steady flowing in the 80F heat.

Climbing the hill was the hard part, not the bad part. The bad part started right at the top of the hill. The storm cell causing all the high winds arrived and brought with it even stronger winds and sideways rain. I have no idea how fast the wind was blowing but it brought out a primal fear in me. Biking was impossible, holding my bike upright while standing was extremely difficult. I started to worry about the possibility of a tornado, and with nothing to shelter beneath/next to for miles in either direction, I laid down in the ditch along the road until things settled down. Thankfully, there was no tornado, the storm blew over after 5 minutes and the regularly schedule 30mph winds resumed. This pattern repeated 2 more times over the course of the day.

I finally arrived at my destination for the night, an old church converted into a biker hostel in the ghost town of Jeffery City. I guess this place was a big uranium mining town, but it’s just about empty now with only 47 people living here. The church is pretty creepy, it’s just a bunch of empty rooms that you’re allowed to sleep in. 

I’m ready to leave this desolate wind blown section of the country.
