It feels so strange to be this close to the end. After chipping away at this goal for the past 72 days, I’m only 1 day away from New York City. I’ve been warned about how anticlimactic it can feel to finally cross the finish line on journeys like this. After months of yearning for the end, it eventually comes, then passes, and time carries on. I’m sitting here in my comfortable hotel bed trying to put feelings into words but keep coming up short. I’m nervous about returning to real life and resuming real responsibilities. I’m scared this trip will fade into distant memory and leave me unchanged. I’m overwhelmed by the thought that I might not have this type of time and freedom in the near future. In the same breath, I’m also full of excitement about this new chapter in a new city full of opportunities. In summary, I’m a bit stuck in my head. For now, I’ll return to what feels comfortable, sharing this journey with you.

Today’s ride started early, and for the last time, with Mike. After the final shared miles of the trip, we said our goodbyes and parted ways. I hope to see you back out on the road someday, Mike! The next chunk of milage carried me along a beautiful bike path. The morning fog was thick and felt as though I was pedaling through a cloud. Small lake towns turned to rolling farmland, then to dense forests.

At one point, I glided past a patch of vibrant purple loosestrife (pictured), the sound of my ticking wheels startled a flock of American goldfinches who all took off flying at once (fun fact: a flock of goldfinches is also called a charm). The sight of all the neon yellow birds flying above the foggy purple and green pasture was absolutely captivating. It was one of those moments that you just had to be there for.

All of the sudden, I emerged from the forest and was greeted by a very modern, metal, sculptural style building. I had entered Bard Universities campus. The grounds of this school felt like the New York equivalent of UC Santa Cruz.

Shortly after, unbeknownst to me, my bike computer rerouted my plan for the day. It found a more direct route to Poughkeepsie. Under normal circumstances, I would rejoice in the idea of shaving milage. However, I was really looking forward to seeing the whole length of the Empire State trail. I discovered the change 10 miles too late and decided to carry on instead of turning around. My day was shortened by almost 20 miles, but instead of protected bike paths through beautiful forests, I was sequestered to the dirty shoulder of highway route 9.

I arrived to my hotel in Poughkeepsie much earlier than expected and got both lunch (Greek) and dinner (Mexican) in town, but spent most of the afternoon lounging in the comfy bed. Maybe it’s the lack of movement that has left me feeling down in the dumps. It seems the days I spend the most time in the saddle turn out to be the best ones.

T-1 day until I arrive in NYC
