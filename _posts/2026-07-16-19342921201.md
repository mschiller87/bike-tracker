This morning was GORGEOUS. All of the photos included in today’s update were taken in the first 10 miles of the ride during the stunning sunrise and early morning light.

I’ve said it before and I’ll say it again, I’m in love with the way the sun shines through hazy morning mist. I frequently find myself turning my bike around to attempt to capture or simply re-live the experience of watching rays of amber light peek through gaps in trees.

Almost all of today’s ride was on the Katy trial. It provided the most consistent shade of the trip so far, keeping temperatures a little cooler than outside the tree tunnel. The forest around us was incredibly thick with billowing mounds of green growth covering sides of three, bushes and earth that were exposed to the sun. As we pedal along sections of Lewis and Clark’s original trail, I struggle to imagine what it would be like to traverse this landscape unaided by trails and roads.

Our accommodations for the night is the Turner Katy Bunkhouse. A 40 bed house that is reserved exclusively for hikers and bikers on the Katy trail. We met two other cyclists traveling east from Kansas City to St Louis. A cold shower and shelter from the afternoon thunderstorms were a welcomed treat after a surprisingly difficult 62 mile day. My body is used to the rolling efficiency you get on smooth pavement and it feels pretty rattled after riding on gravel all day. The storms today brought lightning closer than I’ve ever experienced. A strike landed so close to the bunkhouse that there was absolutely no delay in the sound of thunder and sounded more like a bomb going off than thunder.
