I woke up from an amazing 10 hours of sleep. By sleeping indoors, we avoided thunder, lightning, and heavy rain that lasted until the early morning.

A massive raspberry pancake with eggs and bacon by the Madison River at mile 15 was a huge boost to the morale. Around this area, I passed through Earthquake Lake. A magnitude 7.8 earthquake hit this area in 1959 and caused an enormous landslide. The slide unfortunately killed many campers at the site below, and it also dramatically altered the surrounding landscape. The loose earth blocked the road and the river, thus creating Earthquake Lake.

The rest of the ride consisted of rolling hills, lake views, and the sea of humanity that is West Yellowstone. A quick smoked brisket sandwich and a McDonald’s ice cream cone to top off the fuel tanks, and a grocery run to feed me for the next 2 days were all the purchases I made in town.

Shortly after entering Yellowstone National Park, I was forced to address the elephant in the room; I had made it over 1,200 miles without a flat tire. That clock was reset with only 5 miles to go for the day. I’m still new to tubeless tires, I spent half an hour trying to refill, add sealant, and plug a small hole in my tire. I eventually gave up and threw in the only inner tube I had on me to get rolling again. The whole ordeal required me to completely strip my bike and spread out on the side of the road for all of the passing drivers to witness. Fingers crossed these puppies stay full until the next town in 3 days…

I ended the day on a good note with one of the best camp meals I’ve ever made. Smoked brisket ravioli, pesto, tomatoes, and bacon bits. Yes Mom, I like tomatoes now.
