Cute bed and breakfast does not mean comfortable bed and breakfast. I woke up this morning sweating. The house we were staying in was built in the 1800s and didn’t have the best central cooling, it also didn’t help that the host wouldn’t turn the thermostat down below 78 degrees. The furniture in the home was beautiful and of the same era when the home was built. It was all rock hard and creaky. If it wasn’t for the fun game of monopoly we played with the caretaker’s mom and daughter last night, I would have preferred a campground.

I started the day hot and sweaty in bed and continued it hot and sweaty on the bike. The beauty of the Katy trail was starting to loose its luster after three days of sweltering humidity. We stopped at mile 20 at the Bike Stop Cafe, well named, and got some breakfast. I left that stop in a bad place. The heat was taking its tole and I had fallen behind on my hydration. For the last 15 miles of the trail, I felt I like I was suffocating.

We finally emerged from the tree tunnel at the end of the Katy trail and were treated to less humid air and direct overhead sunlight. Rolling on pavement was a welcomed change after 200 miles of packed limestone. It allowed me to glide much further and use less effort with each pedal stroke. I was feeling slightly better after drinking a bunch of water, but my wet 3-days-worth-of-sweat clothing was keeping me far from feeling good. The stink record was set again, I was no longer feeling like a human and was struggling to keep up my act as a functioning member of society.

Around noon, I climbed the Clark Bridge and crossed the Mississippi River into Illinois (my ninth state!). I was welcomed to the state with beautiful PAVED bike trails through LESS HUMID tree tunnels. We ended the day in Edwardsville at Pete’s house. He welcomed us in his home with lunch and dinner, while him and his wife managed their two kids. I immediately hopped in the shower and regained my sanity. It’s incredible how amazing it feels to be inside, clean, and well fed. The people willing to provide this support to touring cyclists are real life super hero’s.
